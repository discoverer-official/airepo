AIRepo — SO-02 Principles, Authority and Ownership
Status: PUBLIC CANONICAL / AIRepo 1.1.0
Public release parent: AIRepo 1.0.0

1. Mission
Own the framework-wide principles for authority, ownership, Systems of Record, evidence, minimality, portability and project precedence.

2. Core principles
- Target-project authority precedence.
- Evidence does not become authority by existence.
- Evidence fitness is claim-relative: exact binding is necessary where material, but exact binding alone does not prove that the verification method can observe the claimed property or boundary.
- Validation artifacts may verify semantics but do not define semantic authority.
- One semantic decision/state class has one Authority Owner.
- One state class has one canonical System of Record; mirrors/caches are explicit.
- Semantic responsibility precedes physical placement.
- Active Consumer Gate and No Speculative Structure apply to AIRepo itself and to consumers.
- External Capability First before new owned subsystems.
- Provider neutrality and technology agnosticism are mandatory for universal semantics.
- Generated representations are non-authoritative projections.

3. Ownership model
Every governed semantic/state class resolves an owner, canonical source/SoR, consumers, durability/lifecycle, access/confidentiality constraints and compatibility obligations where applicable. Filesystem location alone never establishes ownership or authority.

4. Authority model
AIRepo semantic owners define only AIRepo semantics. Target-project Constitutions, accepted ADRs, policies, contracts and other project authorities remain superior in application. A project may adopt AIRepo requirements explicitly, but framework use alone does not create project authority.

5. Evidence model
Repository observations, research, CI, runtime observations, generated output, AI conclusions and examples are evidence. They may justify or challenge a decision but do not approve it unless the declared authority mechanism says so.

Evidence is evaluated against a concrete claim. Evidence that is exact-revision-bound may still be insufficient when the method cannot observe the claimed property, exercises a different boundary, omits a material behavior input, or has an undeclared comparability limitation. Tests, validators, evals, graders, generated inventories and reports may verify a claim but MUST NOT originate, narrow, replace or promote the semantic authority that the claim concerns.

6. AI-agent authority
AI agents may inspect, classify, research, validate, generate derived views and prepare decisions. They may not infer approval, change project authority, self-assign maturity/release state or perform destructive/authority-changing remediation without the required permission.

7. Single-source invariant
Where multiple representations exist, exactly one semantic owner is canonical. Human-readable indexes, catalogs, agent bundles, machine views, evidence matrices and release checklists must derive from or reference that owner rather than restate competing truth.

8. Minimality
Before adding a new location, owner, SoR, manifest, validator, workflow, registry or lifecycle, show the active consumer/failure mode, why existing mechanisms are insufficient, lifecycle ownership and the less irreversible alternative.

9. Confidentiality and safety
Protected data handling remains subject to target-project authority and applicable controls. AIRepo cannot weaken confidentiality, retention or access rules through organization guidance.

10. Composability
AIRepo must compose with other frameworks through explicit boundaries and adapters, never hidden dependencies or ownership overlap. When another Framework owns context composition, governance, continuation, runtime, retrieval or another referenced semantic surface, AIRepo consumes its revision-bound evidence/identity as applicable and does not redefine that surface.
